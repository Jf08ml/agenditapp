If you're looking into how much scheduling software costs for your beauty salon, barbershop or spa, you've probably already made the most important decision: to stop managing your calendar by hand and go digital. What's left is choosing the right tool at the right price.

The good news: the scheduling software market has options for every size of business, from free basic tools to premium platforms with everything included. The bad news: pricing is confusing, plans hide important features, and most of the available options weren't designed for the Colombian market.

This comparison has one clear goal: to give you the real numbers so you can make an informed decision.

## What should you expect from scheduling software?

Before comparing prices, you need to know which features are essential and which are extras you may not need. Here's the list by level of importance:

### Basic features (every solution should have them)

- 24/7 online booking from a phone
- Real-time availability management
- Automatic appointment reminders
- An admin dashboard to view and manage the calendar
- Multiple professionals / workstations

### Important features (they make the difference)

- WhatsApp integration for reminders and confirmations
- Client and service history
- Online payments or deposits (to reduce no-shows)
- Access for your team from any device
- Revenue reports and basic statistics

### Advanced features (useful for larger businesses)

- Your own domain for your booking page
- Points and loyalty program
- Email / WhatsApp marketing to your whole client base
- Product inventory management
- Multiple locations

With this list in hand, you can evaluate each option against what your business really needs.

## The "free" option trap

Many scheduling tools offer free plans as a hook. The reality is that these free plans usually come with limitations that make them impractical for a salon in real operation:

- **Appointment limits**: they only allow 5 or 10 bookings a month. A salon with 10 appointments a day goes over on day one.
- **The software's branding**: your booking link says "yourbusiness.calendly.com" or "yourbusiness.booksy.com" instead of your own brand.
- **No automatic reminders**: the most important feature of any system comes with the paid plan.
- **No client history**: you can't see each client's service history or notes.
- **Transaction fees**: some charge up to 3–5% of every payment processed instead of a flat fee.

A free plan can be useful to try a tool, but for a real business you need a paid plan. The question is how much it should cost you.

## The WhatsApp factor: why it changes the whole price analysis

For a business in Colombia, WhatsApp integration isn't an extra: it's a core feature. Here's the problem with the international options: none of the big platforms has WhatsApp built in natively. Reminders arrive by email or SMS.

What's the email open rate in Colombia? Between 15% and 25%. WhatsApp's? **98%**.

## What you shouldn't pay for in scheduling software

Some pricing practices to watch out for and avoid:

- **Per-appointment charges**: some platforms charge a fee for every booking received. With 300 appointments a month, costs skyrocket.
- **Fees on payments**: if the software takes 2–5% of every payment processed, that can add up to COP 300,000–500,000 (US$75–125) a month for a mid-sized salon.
- **Basic features locked in the premium plan**: if automatic reminders only come with the most expensive plan, the basic plan isn't much use in practice.
- **Mandatory annual contracts**: prefer month-to-month billing so you can switch if the software doesn't meet expectations.
- **Onboarding or setup fees**: you should be able to set up the software yourself. If they charge extra for "implementation", that's a red flag.
